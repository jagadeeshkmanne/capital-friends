How it works, in one picture

How the corpus is calculated

  1. Today's monthly expenses are inflated to the retirement year: ₹60,000 at 6% for 20 years becomes about ₹1.9 lakh a month.
  2. The corpus is sized so that inflation-rising withdrawals last from retirement to the age you choose, while the remaining money earns the post-retirement return. This is more realistic than a flat "25× expenses" rule.
  3. What you have already saved is grown at the pre-retirement return. The gap is converted into a monthly SIP for the years left.

What people get wrong

  • Ignoring inflation. At 6%, prices double every 12 years. A 35-year-old retiring at 55 needs more than 3× today's expenses per month.
  • Counting the house. You will live in it; it does not pay for groceries. Count EPF, PPF, NPS, mutual funds, FDs and stocks.
  • One return for 50 years. Use a higher return while accumulating and a lower one after retiring, when most money should be in safer funds.

After the number: how to actually withdraw

A corpus is only half the plan. The other half is how you draw it without selling equity in a crash. Capital Friends uses three buckets: income (2 years of expenses, safe), stability (next 5 years, hybrid/debt) and growth (the rest, equity). It refills the income bucket from growth in good years and from stability in bad years. Read how it works on the retirement bucket strategy page, or try the SWP calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How much corpus do I need to retire in India?

A common rule is 25–30 times your yearly expenses at retirement (after inflation). For ₹1 lakh a month at retirement that is ₹3–3.6 crore. The calculator above gives a more exact number for your ages and returns.

What withdrawal rate is safe in India?

Around 3.5–4% of the corpus in the first year, rising with inflation. Higher inflation in India argues for the lower end, or for keeping part of the corpus in equity after retiring.

Should I count EPF and NPS?

Yes, they are retirement money. Capital Friends counts EPF, PPF and NPS for the retirement goal only and never includes them in a sell step, since they pay out on their own rules.

What if I cannot invest the SIP the calculator shows?

Start with what you can and step it up every year, push the retirement age a little, or lower planned expenses. Re-run the calculation yearly; small corrections early are far cheaper than big ones late.
