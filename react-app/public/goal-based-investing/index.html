Goal-based investing

A goal is a date and an amount. Capital Friends puts your goals on a road, works out what each costs by its date, counts the money you already have against it, tells you the SIP still needed, and then, year by year, moves that goal's money from equity to safe funds as the date comes near. The money comes from the funds, FDs and EPF you already hold; nothing has to be bought specially.

Creating a goal

  1. Goals → Add Goal. Type (Retirement, Child Education, House, Car, Travel, Wedding, Emergency fund, or Custom), a name, who it is for, and a priority.
  2. The numbers. For retirement: your age now, the age you want to retire, today's monthly expenses, inflation and expected return. For a child's education: the child's age and the course; the app sets the start date itself. For other goals: today's cost and the year.
  3. The app shows the future cost, the corpus needed, and the answer: the monthly SIP from today, or the lump sum needed today, with the suggested equity/debt split for that horizon.
  4. Review and create. The goal appears on the road with its year, have-today, need-today and status.

Retirement: inflation and the safe withdrawal rate

Today's ₹50,000 a month at 7% inflation is ₹93,000 a month in nine years. The corpus has to pay that for the rest of your life, so the app sizes it with a safe withdrawal rate (SWR): the share you can take out in the first year, with the rest staying invested, so the money lasts until 85.

Retire at 52 on ₹93,000 a month and the corpus is about ₹2.8 crore. The retirement corpus calculator runs the same maths without sign-in, and the three-bucket guide explains how the corpus is spent.

Which money is for which goal

You do not link funds to goals one by one. Click Money for goals, and you see every investment of the family. Tick the ones that are for goals in general: your funds, the FD, the EPF; leave out the kids' own portfolios and the house. The app then shares that pool across all goals by itself: the near goal gets the safe money (the FD, the hybrid funds), the far goals get the equity, EPF and PPF go to retirement only. Open Investments on any goal to see exactly which funds and how much of each it uses.

The glide path

A goal ten years away can sit in equity. The same goal one year away should not, because a bad year right before the date cuts the corpus with no time to recover. The glide path is the equity share the app targets for a goal by the years left:

Years to the goalEquity target
more than 1085%
7–1075%
5–765%
3–550%
1–330%
under 110%
Emergency fund0% (all safe)

Retirement eases from 85% to 75% between ten and five years out, and in the last five years follows the bucket plan instead. When a goal's equity is a few points above its path, the goal card shows the step: for example 2029: move ₹4.2 L from the flexicap fund to the FD, with the fund names and units. Steps only trigger while the market is near its high, so you are never told to sell at a low. Goals paid over several years (four years of college fees) glide each year's part separately.

On track or not

Each goal shows Have today (its share of the pool), Need today (what, growing at the expected return, reaches the target by the date) and the gap. On track means nothing to add. If it drifts, the gap is shown as an extra SIP or lump sum, and the daily email carries it until it is closed.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to assign each fund to a goal?

No. Tick which investments are for goals in general; the app shares them, giving near goals the safe money and far goals the equity. You can see and adjust the result on each goal.

How is the retirement corpus calculated?

Today's monthly expenses are inflated to the retirement year, then a safe withdrawal rate that depends on how long the money must last (3% if you retire before 45, 4% around 55, up to 6% after 68) sizes the corpus. The gap after existing savings becomes the SIP needed.

What is a glide path?

The equity share the app targets for a goal by the years left: 85% far out, down to 10% in the last year. As the date comes near it suggests moving a little each year from equity to safe funds, only in good markets.

Does the app move the money?

No. It shows the step with fund names and amounts; you place the switch where you invest and record it.

Can a goal be paid over several years?

Yes. College fees over four years are four parts, each with its own glide path, so the fourth year's money stays in growth longer than the first year's.

Is this investment advice?

No. The plan is computed from your own numbers and standard rules; what you invest in and when is your decision.
