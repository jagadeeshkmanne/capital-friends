The Three-Bucket Retirement Strategy

A retirement corpus is only useful if you can draw from it for 30 years without selling equity at the bottom of a crash. The bucket strategy splits the corpus into three jars by when the money is needed, and refills them with rules instead of nerves.

The three buckets

Income bucket: next 2 years

Two years of expenses in the safest place: liquid fund, short-duration debt, sweep-in FD. Your monthly expenses come only from here.

Stability bucket: years 3 to 7

Five years of expenses in hybrid and debt funds, plus EPF, PPF and NPS which are never sold but count here. Refills income when the market is down.

Growth bucket: everything else

Equity funds. Left alone to grow; refills the other two only in good years, when the market is near its high.

How the refills work

  • Five years before retirement: every quarter a small amount moves from growth to stability, and in the last two years to income too, but only in a good market, so nothing is sold in one go or at a low.
  • Every month after retiring: expenses are paid from the income bucket. The other two are not touched. Bucket sizes use this year's expenses, so they grow with inflation.
  • Good year (market near its high): growth refills income and tops up stability.
  • Bad year (growth funds more than 10% below their high): growth is left alone; stability refills income.
  • Recovery year: once the market is back near its high, growth refills both.
  • Long fall: stability never goes below two years of expenses; the app warns you to spend a little less for a while.

Frequently asked questions

Why not just an SWP from a balanced fund?

An SWP sells units every month regardless of the market. Buckets make sure the units sold in a crash come from the safe jar, so equity gets time to recover.

How big should the income bucket be?

Two years of expenses is the common choice; it covers most bear markets. Some retirees keep three.

Where do EPF, PPF and NPS fit?

They count in the stability bucket but are never switched or sold by the app; they pay out on their own rules. When one matures, you press Received and the money joins the plan.

Is this advice?

No. It is a rule-based way to organise withdrawals that you can read and change. Capital Friends is a tracker, not a SEBI-registered adviser.
