How the goal calculation works

  1. Today's cost is inflated to the goal year: ₹25 lakh at 8% for 10 years is about ₹54 lakh.
  2. Money already saved is grown at the expected return and subtracted.
  3. The gap is converted into a monthly SIP (or a one-time investment today) at the expected return.

Which return to use for which goal

  • Under 3 years: debt or arbitrage funds, 6–7%. Equity is too risky this close.
  • 3–7 years: hybrid or balanced advantage, 9–10%.
  • 7+ years: mostly equity, 11–12%, moving to safer funds as the date comes near.

The glide path: moving money as the goal comes near

A goal that is 100% equity three months before college is a gamble. Capital Friends gives each goal a road: far goals get the equity, near goals get the safe money, and the app shows how much to shift each year so the money is safe by the time you need it. For a child's education it even sets the date from the child's age and the course.

Frequently asked questions

What inflation rate should I use for education?

Private college fees in India have risen 8–10% a year; use 10% for professional courses and foreign education, 8% for general degrees.

Can one SIP serve several goals?

You can invest in one place and let a tracker split it. Capital Friends shares your investments across all goals automatically: the emergency fund and near goals get the safe money, far goals get the equity.

Is a lumpsum better than a SIP for a goal?

If you have the money now, investing it today needs much less than a SIP spread over the years, because it compounds longer. The calculator shows both numbers.

When should I move a goal from equity to debt?

Start about 5 years before the date and shift a little each year, preferably in good market years, so you are never forced to sell in a crash.
